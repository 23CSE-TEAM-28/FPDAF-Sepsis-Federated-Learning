Clinical Urgency & Technological Gaps

The Challenge: Surviving Sepsis Across Siloed Hospitals

Sepsis affects over 49 million patients worldwide, claiming 11 million lives each year. In intensive care, mortality escalates by 7.6%–8.0% for every single hour of delayed treatment. Deploying AI at scale, however, is obstructed by three fundamental hurdles:

1. Data Sovereignty & Privacy

Strict clinical regulations (HIPAA, GDPR, India's DPDPA) strictly prohibit pooling raw 40-biomarker ICU records into a centralized cloud server, creating impenetrable institutional data silos.

Solution: Decentralized FL

2. Inter-Hospital Non-IID Drift

Community hospitals treat geriatric patients (mean age 68.4y), trauma centers experience acute hemodynamic volatility, and academic centers care for complex oncology cases. Standard FedAvg suffers catastrophic gradient conflict.

Solution: AD-CUSUM Detection

3. Edge Bandwidth Exhaustion

Bi-level personalized federated baselines (e.g., Ditto) transmit continuous bidirectional multi-megabyte parameter tensors, saturating hospital edge firewalls with >2.48 GB of communication payload per 50 rounds.

Solution: CSSP 0-Upload Freezing

Change-Point Detection

Adaptive Divergence-Weighted Cumulative Sum (AD-CUSUM)

Traditional CUSUM change-point detectors rely on scalar residuals and static thresholds, causing frequent false alarms under noisy sensor dropouts. AD-CUSUM couples loss residuals with symmetric Jensen-Shannon Divergence ($D_{\text{JSD}}$) and a gradient variance damping factor:

$$S_r = \max\Big(0, S_{r-1} + w_{\text{grad}}^{(r)} \cdot \big((\mathcal{L}_{\text{val}, r} - \mu_0) + \beta D_{\text{JSD}}^{(r)} - \kappa_r\big)\Big)$$
  • Gradient Variance Damping: $w_{\text{grad}}^{(r)} = 1 + \tanh(\gamma \cdot \text{Var}(\nabla_{\theta_k} \mathcal{L}))$, damping false triggers from mini-batch noise.
  • Self-Calibrating Slack: $\kappa_r = \mu_{\text{loss}} + \alpha \sigma_{\text{loss}}$, automatically adapting to moving error variance.
  • Calibrated Threshold: $H = 3.0$ triggers selective edge adaptation when true clinical population shifts occur.

Personalized Head Optimization

Dynamic Dual-Phase Saliency & Entropy-Regularized Personalization

Naive fine-tuning upon drift induces catastrophic forgetting of shared representations learned across other medical centers. DDP-ERP decouples the client head $\phi^k$ and optimizes a 4-term regularized objective:

$$\mathcal{L}_{\text{DDP-ERP}} = \mathcal{L}_{\text{CE}}(y, \hat{y}) + \lambda_1 \mathcal{L}_{\text{distill}} + \lambda_2 \mathcal{L}_{\text{align}} + \lambda_3 \mathcal{H}(P_{\text{local}})$$
  • Focal Weighted Cross-Entropy: $w_{\text{pos}} = 5.0$ combats severe 1:55 sepsis class imbalance.
  • Knowledge Distillation ($\mathcal{L}_{\text{distill}}$): $D_{\text{KL}}(P_{\text{local}} \,\|\, P_{\text{global}})$ bounds divergence from global consensus.
  • Cosine Parameter Alignment ($\mathcal{L}_{\text{align}}$): $1 - \frac{\langle \phi^k, \theta_{\text{global}}^{\text{head}} \rangle}{\|\phi^k\|_2 \cdot \|\theta_{\text{global}}^{\text{head}}\|_2}$ preserves global trajectory alignment.
  • Entropy Regularization ($\mathcal{H}$): Prevents overconfident classification under missing ICU inputs.

Edge Communication Efficiency

Client-Side Selective Personalization (CSSP)

In clinical federated networks, edge firewalls and WAN throttles make high-volume model handshakes impractical. Under CSSP, the communication burden is eradicated:

$$\nabla_\theta \mathcal{L} \leftarrow 0 \quad \text{and} \quad \text{Upload}(\phi^k) = \emptyset$$
Ditto PFL (Bi-Level Baseline): 2.48 GB
FPDAF-v2 (CSSP Edge Freezing): 1.52 GB (-38.2%)
  • Backbone Freezing: Shared BiLSTM-Attention weights are locked during local adaptation.
  • Zero Upload: Client heads $\phi^k$ remain on-premise, preserving absolute patient privacy.
  • 960 MB Payload Saved: Keeps adaptation latency under 6 minutes on standard hospital hardware.

Claim 1: Distributed Federated Sepsis Forecasting Architecture

INDEPENDENT SYSTEM CLAIM
A privacy-preserving distributed computing system for forecasting clinical sepsis in intensive care patients across a plurality of decentralized hospital client nodes, comprising: a decoupled neural architecture featuring a shared temporal feature representation backbone (BiLSTM and Multi-Head Self-Attention) and client-specific personalized prediction heads ($\phi^k$); wherein each client node ingests 40 continuous time-series physiological biomarkers without transmitting raw patient records off-premises under HIPAA and DPDPA mandates.

Claim 2: AD-CUSUM Dynamic Change-Point Detection

DEPENDENT CLAIM (TO CLAIM 1)
The system of claim 1, wherein each client node executes an Adaptive Divergence-Weighted Cumulative Sum (AD-CUSUM) change-point detector tracking cumulative drift score $S_r = \max(0, S_{r-1} + w_{\text{grad}}^{(r)} \cdot ((\mathcal{L}_{\text{val}, r} - \mu_0) + \beta D_{\text{JSD}}^{(r)} - \kappa_r))$, wherein symmetric Jensen-Shannon divergence $D_{\text{JSD}}^{(r)}$ quantifies prediction probability distribution shifts.

Claim 3: Gradient Variance Damping Factor

DEPENDENT CLAIM (TO CLAIM 2)
The system of claim 2, wherein said detector dynamically scales accumulated residuals using a gradient variance damping factor $w_{\text{grad}}^{(r)} = 1 + \tanh(\gamma \cdot \text{Var}(\nabla_{\theta_k} \mathcal{L}))$, dampening spurious alarms generated by high mini-batch gradient variance and transient ICU sensor dropouts.

Claim 4: Self-Calibrating Slack Coefficient $\kappa_r$

DEPENDENT CLAIM (TO CLAIM 2)
The system of claim 2, wherein the slack coefficient $\kappa_r = \mu_{\text{loss}} + \alpha \sigma_{\text{loss}}$ continuously updates based on a sliding window of historical validation errors, preventing drift accumulator explosion during standard model convergence.

Claim 5: DDP-ERP Four-Term Regularized Personalization Objective

INDEPENDENT METHOD CLAIM
A computer-implemented method for adapting a personalized neural network upon drift detection, comprising optimizing an objective: $\mathcal{L}_{\text{DDP-ERP}} = \mathcal{L}_{\text{CE}}(y, \hat{y}) + \lambda_1 \mathcal{L}_{\text{distill}} + \lambda_2 \mathcal{L}_{\text{align}} + \lambda_3 \mathcal{H}(P_{\text{local}})$, preventing catastrophic forgetting of shared multi-center representations while adapting to local ICU patient demographics.

Claim 6: Knowledge Distillation Bounded Divergence

DEPENDENT CLAIM (TO CLAIM 5)
The method of claim 5, wherein the distillation loss comprises Kullback-Leibler divergence $D_{\text{KL}}(P_{\text{local}} \,\|\, P_{\text{global}})$ penalizing deviation of the client prediction distribution from the aggregated global consensus model.

Claim 7: Cosine Head Parameter Alignment

DEPENDENT CLAIM (TO CLAIM 5)
The method of claim 5, wherein the alignment loss penalizes angular divergence $1 - \frac{\langle \phi^k, \theta_{\text{global}}^{\text{head}} \rangle}{\|\phi^k\|_2 \cdot \|\theta_{\text{global}}^{\text{head}}\|_2}$, constraining edge optimization within a bounded directional hypercone.

Claim 8: Shannon Entropy Calibration Under Missingness

DEPENDENT CLAIM (TO CLAIM 5)
The method of claim 5, wherein Shannon entropy $\mathcal{H}(P_{\text{local}}) = -\sum_c P_c \log P_c$ penalizes overconfident predictions when vital laboratory inputs exhibit high clinical sparsity.

Claim 9: Client-Side Selective Personalization (CSSP) Freezing Protocol

INDEPENDENT PROTOCOL CLAIM
A communication protocol for edge federated networks, wherein upon cumulative drift score $S_r \ge H$, the client node locks gradient backpropagation into the shared temporal backbone ($\nabla_\theta \mathcal{L} \leftarrow 0$) and completely suppresses parameter transmission ($\text{Upload}(\phi^k) = \emptyset$), saving 38.2% wide-area network bandwidth.

Claim 10: Zero-Upload Edge Parameter Sovereignty

DEPENDENT CLAIM (TO CLAIM 9)
The protocol of claim 9, wherein personalized head parameters $\phi^k$ reside exclusively on local hospital storage, preventing membership inference and model inversion attacks.

Claim 11: Multi-Head Self-Attention Early Warning Window

DEPENDENT CLAIM (TO CLAIM 1)
The system of claim 1, wherein multi-head self-attention computes temporal attribution across 24 historical hours, identifying clinical risk accumulation 5.8 hours prior to physiological septic shock onset.

Claim 12: Point-of-Care Bedside Counterfactual Recourse

DEPENDENT CLAIM (TO CLAIM 1)
The system of claim 1, further comprising a counterfactual recourse engine calculating physiological perturbations required to invert predicted septic shock risk $\hat{y} \ge 0.5$ to safe baseline status $\hat{y} < 0.2$.

Claim 13: Automated Surviving Sepsis Campaign (SSC) Bundle Dispatch

DEPENDENT CLAIM (TO CLAIM 12)
The system of claim 12, wherein predicted risk exceeding calibrated thresholds automatically dispatches Surviving Sepsis Campaign (SSC) Hour-1 electronic clinical orders: stat blood lactate, 2 sets of blood cultures, empiric broad-spectrum antibiotics, and 30 mL/kg crystalloid resuscitation.

Claim 14: Non-Transitory Computer-Readable Storage Medium

DEPENDENT MEDIA CLAIM
A non-transitory computer-readable storage medium embodying executable instructions causing one or more distributed hospital processors to execute the methods of claims 1 through 13.

Claim 15: Heterogeneous Multi-Center ICU Topology

DEPENDENT TOPOLOGY CLAIM
The system of claim 1, configured across at least three heterogeneous clinical tiers: a primary community hospital, a specialized acute trauma resuscitation center, and a tertiary academic oncology research hospital.

Bridging the Trust Gap

The Three Pillars of Clinical Explainability (XAI)

Intensivists distrust opaque black-box neural networks. FPDAF structures explainability into three actionable clinical questions:

Pillar 1: Temporal

"WHEN Did Risk Accumulate?"

The 24-hour Multi-Head Self-Attention distribution reveals the exact historical timeline where risk accumulated. Attention peaks dramatically at 19.8% at Hour 18, providing an automated 5.8-hour advance warning before clinical shock.

Pillar 2: Attribution

"WHAT Biomarkers Drove Risk?"

Integrated Gradients and SHAP decompose neural activations into physiological factors: Heart Rate (35%), Systolic BP (28%), and SpO$_2$ (22%), strictly aligning with international SIRS/qSOFA criteria.

Heart Rate (Tachycardia) 35%
Systolic BP (Hypotension) 28%
SpO2 (Hypoxemia) 22%
Pillar 3: Recourse

"HOW Can Clinicians Intervene?"

Counterfactual Recourse models therapeutic interventions: simulating a $30\text{ mL/kg}$ crystalloid fluid bolus that restores MAP from 58 to 78 mmHg reduces predicted risk from 84% to <20%, offering direct clinical recourse.

↓ 30 mL/kg Crystalloid Resuscitation ↓
Post-Intervention: 18.4% Baseline
