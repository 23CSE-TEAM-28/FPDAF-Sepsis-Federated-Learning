Clinical Urgency & Technological Gaps

The Challenge: Surviving Sepsis Across Siloed Hospitals

Sepsis affects over 49 million patients worldwide, claiming 11 million lives each year. In intensive care, mortality escalates by 7.6%–8.0% for every single hour of delayed treatment. Deploying AI at scale, however, is obstructed by three fundamental hurdles:

1. Data Sovereignty & Privacy

Strict clinical regulations (HIPAA, GDPR, India's DPDPA) strictly prohibit pooling raw 40-biomarker ICU records into a centralized cloud server, creating impenetrable institutional data silos.

Solution: Decentralized FL

2. Inter-Hospital Non-IID Drift

Community hospitals treat geriatric patients (mean age 68.4y), trauma centers experience acute hemodynamic volatility, and academic centers care for complex oncology cases. Standard FedAvg suffers catastrophic gradient conflict.

Solution: AD-CUSUM Detection

3. Edge Bandwidth Exhaustion

Bi-level personalized federated baselines (e.g., Ditto) transmit continuous bidirectional multi-megabyte parameter tensors, saturating hospital edge firewalls with >2.48 GB of communication payload per 50 rounds.

Solution: CSSP 0-Upload Freezing

Change-Point Detection

Adaptive Divergence-Weighted Cumulative Sum (AD-CUSUM)

Traditional CUSUM change-point detectors rely on scalar residuals and static thresholds, causing frequent false alarms under noisy sensor dropouts. AD-CUSUM couples loss residuals with symmetric Jensen-Shannon Divergence ($D_{\text{JSD}}$) and a gradient variance damping factor:

$$S_r = \max\Big(0, S_{r-1} + w_{\text{grad}}^{(r)} \cdot \big((\mathcal{L}_{\text{val}, r} - \mu_0) + \beta D_{\text{JSD}}^{(r)} - \kappa_r\big)\Big)$$
  • Gradient Variance Damping: $w_{\text{grad}}^{(r)} = 1 + \tanh(\gamma \cdot \text{Var}(\nabla_{\theta_k} \mathcal{L}))$, damping false triggers from mini-batch noise.
  • Self-Calibrating Slack: $\kappa_r = \mu_{\text{loss}} + \alpha \sigma_{\text{loss}}$, automatically adapting to moving error variance.
  • Calibrated Threshold: $H = 3.0$ triggers selective edge adaptation when true clinical population shifts occur.

Personalized Head Optimization

Dynamic Dual-Phase Saliency & Entropy-Regularized Personalization

Naive fine-tuning upon drift induces catastrophic forgetting of shared representations learned across other medical centers. DDP-ERP decouples the client head $\phi^k$ and optimizes a 4-term regularized objective:

$$\mathcal{L}_{\text{DDP-ERP}} = \mathcal{L}_{\text{CE}}(y, \hat{y}) + \lambda_1 \mathcal{L}_{\text{distill}} + \lambda_2 \mathcal{L}_{\text{align}} + \lambda_3 \mathcal{H}(P_{\text{local}})$$
  • Focal Weighted Cross-Entropy: $w_{\text{pos}} = 5.0$ combats severe 1:55 sepsis class imbalance.
  • Knowledge Distillation ($\mathcal{L}_{\text{distill}}$): $D_{\text{KL}}(P_{\text{local}} \,\|\, P_{\text{global}})$ bounds divergence from global consensus.
  • Cosine Parameter Alignment ($\mathcal{L}_{\text{align}}$): $1 - \frac{\langle \phi^k, \theta_{\text{global}}^{\text{head}} \rangle}{\|\phi^k\|_2 \cdot \|\theta_{\text{global}}^{\text{head}}\|_2}$ preserves global trajectory alignment.
  • Entropy Regularization ($\mathcal{H}$): Prevents overconfident classification under missing ICU inputs.

Edge Communication Efficiency

Client-Side Selective Personalization (CSSP)

In clinical federated networks, edge firewalls and WAN throttles make high-volume model handshakes impractical. Under CSSP, the communication burden is eradicated:

$$\nabla_\theta \mathcal{L} \leftarrow 0 \quad \text{and} \quad \text{Upload}(\phi^k) = \emptyset$$

Bridging the Trust Gap

The Three Pillars of Clinical Explainability (XAI)

Intensivists distrust opaque black-box neural networks. FPDAF structures explainability into three actionable questions:

Pillar 1: Temporal

"WHEN Did Risk Accumulate?"

The 24-hour Multi-Head Self-Attention distribution reveals the exact historical timeline where risk accumulated. Attention peaks dramatically at 19.8% at Hour 18, providing an automated 5.8-hour advance warning before clinical shock.

Pillar 2: Attribution

"WHAT Biomarkers Drove Risk?"

Integrated Gradients and SHAP decompose neural activations into physiological factors: Heart Rate (35%), Systolic BP (28%), and SpO$_2$ (22%), strictly aligning with international SIRS/qSOFA criteria.

Pillar 3: Recourse

"HOW Can Clinicians Intervene?"

Counterfactual Recourse models therapeutic interventions: simulating a $30\text{ mL/kg}$ crystalloid fluid bolus that restores MAP from 58 to 78 mmHg reduces predicted risk from 84% to <20%, offering direct clinical recourse.

Production Workstation Interfaces

Clinical Decision Support System (CDSS) Portals

Dedicated 3-tier presentation interfaces engineered for intensive care physicians and hospital MLOps administrators.

Doctor Bedside Portal: Real-time bedside monitoring showing 24-hour hemodynamic trend charts, risk dial gauge, temporal self-attention timeline, and automated Surviving Sepsis Campaign (SSC) Hour-1 order dispatching.
MLOps Administrative Portal: Real-time cumulative AD-CUSUM drift telemetry ($S_r$ tracking across hospital nodes), communication round bandwidth logs, and 6-way ROC curve comparison overlays.
Bedside Real-Time AD-CUSUM Concept Drift Telemetry Interface: Continuous tracking of hospital-specific prediction entropy divergence ($D_{\text{JSD}}$) and gradient variance damping at the point of care.
